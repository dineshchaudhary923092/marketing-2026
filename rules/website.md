# Skill: Publishing 18 New Craftnotion Dribbble Work Posts

## Purpose

This skill defines how to plan, package, write, and publish 18 new Dribbble work posts for Craftnotion in a way that strengthens:

- premium positioning
- profile consistency
- service clarity
- portfolio credibility
- inquiry intent
- traffic flow toward Craftnotion

This is not just about posting visuals.  
This is about making the Dribbble profile feel like a serious, curated lead-generation asset for a design and development company.

---

## Core Goal

Every new post should help the viewer understand at least one of these:

- what Craftnotion designs and builds
- the quality of Craftnotion’s execution
- the type of clients or industries Craftnotion works with
- the kind of digital problems Craftnotion solves
- why Craftnotion is worth exploring further

Each post should contribute to the bigger goal:

**Turn the Dribbble profile into a premium portfolio system that builds trust, attracts the right clients, and supports website visits or inquiry intent.**

---

## Positioning to Maintain

All 18 posts must collectively position Craftnotion as:

- a premium digital product and software company
- strong in both design and development
- capable of websites, web apps, mobile apps, SaaS platforms, and MVPs
- practical, business-aware, and execution-focused
- modern without trying too hard
- credible for startups, SMEs, and growth-stage businesses

Do not position Craftnotion as:

- just a UI designer
- just a visual designer
- just a website designer
- a freelance portfolio
- a generic agency that does “everything”

The profile should feel curated, intentional, and commercially relevant.

---

## The Role of the 18 Posts

The 18 posts must work as a system, not as isolated uploads.

Together, they should:

- showcase service breadth
- showcase industry range
- showcase visual quality
- show product-thinking depth
- create consistent trust signals
- improve profile cohesion
- support clicks to website / profile exploration
- help viewers quickly understand what Craftnotion actually does

If all 18 posts look good individually but fail to communicate service fit, they are just expensive decorations.

---

## Publishing Philosophy

The goal is not to dump 18 images online.

The goal is to make the profile feel like:

- a sharp design portfolio
- a serious business portfolio
- a clean visual proof system
- a place where potential clients can understand both aesthetics and capability

Think like a curator, not a content machine.

---

## Content Mix Rule for 18 Posts

The 18 posts should be balanced across categories.

### Recommended Mix

#### 1. Website Showcase Posts
**Target: 5 to 6 posts**
Use for:
- premium business websites
- landing pages
- corporate websites
- real estate sites
- education or brand sites

Goal:
- show visual polish
- show layout quality
- show brand clarity
- position Craftnotion as capable of premium website work

#### 2. SaaS / Product UI Posts
**Target: 4 to 5 posts**
Use for:
- dashboards
- web apps
- product flows
- admin panels
- complex UI systems

Goal:
- show depth beyond websites
- position Craftnotion as a product and software company
- attract SaaS and startup clients

#### 3. Mobile App Posts
**Target: 2 to 3 posts**
Use for:
- app screens
- mobile product flows
- app dashboards
- service apps

Goal:
- show cross-platform capability
- make the profile feel broader and stronger
- signal product versatility

#### 4. MVP / Startup Product Posts
**Target: 2 to 3 posts**
Use for:
- startup-focused launches
- lean product builds
- early-stage digital products

Goal:
- attract startup founders
- highlight clarity, speed, and execution
- show that Craftnotion understands product launch realities

#### 5. Brand / UI System / Concept Support Posts
**Target: 1 to 2 posts**
Use for:
- design system snippets
- component systems
- UI language
- visual identity paired with digital interface design

Goal:
- support depth
- show process maturity
- break visual monotony

---

## Do Not Let the 18 Posts Become Repetitive

Avoid these mistakes:

- all posts looking like dashboard clones
- all captions sounding identical
- all covers using the same color balance
- every post being “clean modern ui design”
- same framing repeated 18 times
- no distinction between websites, SaaS, and apps
- every project sounding equally vague

Viewers should feel progression and range as they browse.

---

## Sequencing Strategy for the 18 Posts

The order of posts matters.

### Recommended Publishing Sequence Logic

#### Phase 1: Establish quality and range
Start with:
- strongest website post
- strongest SaaS/product post
- strongest visually premium project
- one project that clearly signals business credibility

Goal:
- instantly improve profile perception
- make the top rows look intentional

#### Phase 2: Expand capability signals
Then introduce:
- mobile app work
- dashboard / product flows
- startup/MVP-related work
- industry-specific work

Goal:
- show Craftnotion is not one-dimensional

#### Phase 3: Deepen expertise
Then add:
- more complex product work
- more strategic website work
- any UI system / structured design post
- supporting niche projects

Goal:
- create a fuller portfolio identity
- strengthen trust and relevance

---

## Visual Packaging Rules

Every post must feel polished and intentional.

### Must do
- use strong, premium cover compositions
- ensure the first frame is visually powerful
- use consistent Craftnotion-level presentation quality
- keep layout spacing clean
- maintain strong hierarchy
- use real project visuals where possible
- keep branding subtle but visible where relevant

### Avoid
- cluttered compositions
- too much text on visuals
- noisy mockups
- fake 3D for the sake of looking trendy
- random gradients that do not match the project
- overdecorated presentations
- presentation slides that feel like cheap ad creatives

Dribbble is visual-first.  
If the image packaging is weak, the caption will not save it.

---

## Post Structure Rule

Each post should be built like this:

### 1. Cover / First Frame
Purpose:
- stop scrolling
- create curiosity
- instantly signal quality

### 2. Supporting Frames
Purpose:
- show more depth
- show layout variations
- show product areas or sections
- support the case that the project is real and thoughtful

### 3. Optional Final Frame
Purpose:
- subtly reinforce Craftnotion brand
- optionally include website mention or soft CTA
- optionally hint at service category

Do not make the last frame look like a desperate ad.

---

## Caption Strategy

Each caption should follow a consistent structure.

### Recommended Caption Structure
1. **Project hook**
2. **What the project is**
3. **What it was designed to achieve**
4. **Optional relevance/service line**
5. **Soft CTA**

### Example Flow
- project name or context
- what kind of product/site/app it is
- what design/development value it provides
- what Craftnotion does generally
- invitation to explore more or connect

---

## Caption Rules

### Must do
- keep captions professional and readable
- clearly identify the project or category
- provide useful context
- mention business relevance where natural
- include one soft CTA
- keep tone premium and calm
- help viewers understand what the work represents

### Must avoid
- overexplaining the project
- generic praise of your own work
- too many emojis
- fake urgency
- spammy CTA language
- keyword stuffing
- sounding like a social media growth guru

The caption should support the post, not wrestle it to the ground.

---

## CTA Rules for Dribbble Posts

Every post should have a soft CTA.

### CTA goals
- encourage profile exploration
- encourage website visits
- encourage inquiry intent
- strengthen service awareness

### Approved CTA directions
- explore more work on our profile
- visit Craftnotion to see more
- looking to build something similar? let’s talk
- more case studies and product work available on our website
- we design and build websites, apps, and digital products for growing businesses

### Avoid CTA language like
- DM now
- hire us today
- book now
- limited slots
- don’t miss out
- grow fast
- unlock explosive growth

That kind of line makes premium work look like it came with a coupon code.

---

## Website / Backlink Rule

Yes, Dribbble posts should support traffic flow back to Craftnotion.

### Rule
Use soft website references in captions when natural:
- visit Craftnotion to explore more
- more work available through the link in profile
- explore more case studies on our website

### Important
Do not dump raw links into captions unless the presentation still feels clean.
Usually:
- profile link should carry the main outbound path
- captions should support curiosity and intent
- the overall profile should do the selling

---

## Tagging Rules

Tags should help discovery but remain relevant.

### Must do
- use tags relevant to the actual post
- combine broad and niche tags
- include service-related tags
- include platform-related tags
- include project-type tags
- include industry tags where relevant

### Avoid
- irrelevant trend tags
- tag stuffing
- vague tags that do not fit the work
- copying the same exact tag set for all 18 posts

Each post should have a tailored tag group.

---

## Alt Text / Accessibility Rule

If alt text is used, it should:
- describe the visual clearly
- include project type
- mention Craftnotion when natural
- stay readable and accurate
- not become keyword soup

The goal is clarity, not SEO vandalism.

---

## Title Rules

Each post title should:
- be concise
- identify the project or presentation type
- sound premium and intentional
- help the viewer understand what they are seeing

### Good title directions
- Project Name — SaaS Dashboard Design
- Project Name — Website Design
- Project Name — Mobile App UI
- FinTech Platform — Product Design
- Real Estate Website — UI Presentation

### Avoid
- generic titles like “Modern Landing Page”
- ultra-vague titles like “Creative Concept”
- hype titles like “Revolutionary Experience”

---

## Tone Rules for Captions

The caption tone must be:
- calm
- professional
- premium
- business-aware
- design-aware
- slightly confident
- never loud

It should feel like a capable company presenting work, not a teenager begging the algorithm for affection.

---

## Service Signaling Rule

Across the 18 posts, the captions should collectively make it obvious that Craftnotion works on:

- websites
- web applications
- SaaS platforms
- mobile apps
- MVPs
- digital product design

Do not assume viewers will infer this automatically from visuals.

Subtle service signaling in captions matters.

---

## Industry Signaling Rule

Where relevant, use captions and project framing to show industry range.

Suggested industries to highlight through the 18 posts:
- fintech
- healthcare
- education
- real estate
- business software / SaaS
- startup / MVP

This helps potential clients self-identify with the work.

---

## Consistency Rules Across All 18 Posts

Make sure these remain consistent:

- tone of voice
- quality standard
- CTA softness
- title style
- caption structure
- brand positioning
- service names
- professionalism

The profile should feel like one coherent body of work.

---

## Variation Rules Across All 18 Posts

Make sure these vary:

- post type
- visual layout
- industry context
- service emphasis
- color balance
- caption hooks
- supporting frames
- CTA phrasing

Consistency in quality, variation in expression.

---

## Posting Cadence Rule

Do not upload all 18 back to back like a panicked content warehouse.

### Recommended cadence
- 2 to 3 posts per week if you want momentum
- maintain spacing so each post gets breathing room
- keep the top of the profile visually balanced
- review how new posts affect row composition

The profile grid matters.  
Treat it like a portfolio wall, not a dump folder.

---

## Review Checklist Before Publishing Any Post

Before publishing, check:

### Visual Review
- Is the first frame strong enough?
- Does the post feel premium?
- Does it clearly represent the project?
- Does it avoid clutter?
- Does it look consistent with the Craftnotion brand?

### Caption Review
- Does the caption identify the project clearly?
- Does it add context without rambling?
- Does it support positioning?
- Does it include a soft CTA?
- Does it avoid sounding salesy?

### Portfolio Review
- Does this post add something new to the profile?
- Does it improve service clarity?
- Does it improve industry range?
- Does it fit the current profile sequence well?

If the answer is no, revise it before posting.

---

## Final Quality Standard

A post is ready only if it does all of the following:

- looks premium
- feels intentional
- supports brand positioning
- contributes to the portfolio system
- helps a viewer understand Craftnotion better
- supports soft conversion behavior
- does not cheapen the profile

---

## One-Line Summary

**Publish the 18 Dribbble posts as a curated portfolio system that demonstrates range, quality, and business relevance while gently driving viewers toward profile exploration, website visits, and inquiry intent.**
