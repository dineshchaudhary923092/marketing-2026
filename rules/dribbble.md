# Craftnotion Skill — Dribbble Post Strategy, Writing, and Packaging

## Purpose

Use this skill whenever creating, rewriting, reviewing, or sequencing Dribbble posts for Craftnotion.

The goal is not to make posts look "designed for designers." The goal is to make the profile look like a premium, credible, high-signal digital partner that serious clients would trust for websites, SaaS products, dashboards, apps, and MVPs.

This skill should help every post do at least one of the following:

- strengthen Craftnotion's positioning
- improve discoverability on Dribbble
- increase profile visits
- encourage website visits
- create inquiry intent
- make the portfolio feel sharper, more curated, and more commercially useful

---

## Core Positioning

Craftnotion should come across as:

- a premium but practical design and development company
- strongest in SaaS, dashboards, websites, web apps, mobile apps, and MVPs
- modern, business-aware, and execution-focused
- capable of both product thinking and polished visual design
- a serious partner for startups, SMEs, and growth-stage businesses

Craftnotion should **not** come across as:

- a random collection of pretty shots
- a freelancer account pretending to be a studio
- a vague "we do everything" agency
- an overexcited AI-era buzzword machine
- a portfolio written for other agencies instead of buyers

---

## Dribbble Strategy Principle

On Dribbble:

- visuals earn the click
- clarity earns the save
- positioning earns the inquiry
- proof earns the deal

So every post should sell:

1. visual quality
2. category clarity
3. business relevance
4. curiosity to explore more

Do not write like every viewer is a designer.
Write like some viewers are founders, operators, product people, and business owners.

---

## What Every Post Must Include

Each finalized post should include these 5 items:

1. **Title**
2. **Content / Caption**
3. **CTA**
4. **Image Alt Text**
5. **Tags**

This format stays fixed.
The writing style does not.

---

## Universal Writing Rules

### Must do

- Write in clear, direct English
- Sound modern, smart, and commercially aware
- Use natural B2B language
- Make the category obvious quickly
- Keep the caption readable and scannable
- Let the writing support the visual instead of overpowering it
- Make the post feel intentional, not mass-generated
- Use a soft CTA that fits naturally at the end

### Must avoid

- repetitive challenge-solution-result template on every post
- empty adjectives doing all the work
- fake-premium words stacked on top of each other
- "innovative / cutting-edge / world-class / revolutionary" filler
- startup-bro lines
- pushy CTAs
- desperate urgency
- generic agency sludge
- paragraphs so long they feel like a case study trapped in the wrong app

---

## Caption Length Rules

Do **not** force every post into the same caption length.

### Use 1 paragraph for:

- website showcases
- landing pages
- concept shots
- visual-first posts
- UI section shots
- lighter client work

### Use 2 short paragraphs for:

- SaaS products
- dashboards
- fintech
- multi-role platforms
- workflow systems
- product experiences that need business context

### Use 1 paragraph + 1 short closing line for:

- opinion-led posts
- premium website work
- posts where a single sharp line improves memorability

Rule:
**Fixed format, flexible rhythm.**

---

## Approved Caption Modes

Use these modes to create controlled variety across the profile.
Do not invent a new style every time.
Do not use one style for everything.

### Mode 1 — Business-first
Best for: websites, service brands, premium corporate sites

Approach:
- start from the business need
- explain what the website had to achieve
- show how the digital direction improves clarity, trust, or conversion
- close cleanly

Tone:
- practical
- sharp
- commercially aware

Example opening style:
- "A business website does not need more decoration. It needs more clarity where decisions happen."

### Mode 2 — Product-first
Best for: SaaS, dashboards, admin panels, fintech, workflow tools

Approach:
- start from product complexity or user friction
- focus on workflow, visibility, structure, usability, decision support
- make the product feel purposeful
- avoid brochure language

Tone:
- intelligent
- product-aware
- functional

Example opening style:
- "Complex products do not need more visual effects. They need better visibility."

### Mode 3 — Experience-first
Best for: mobile apps, login flows, consumer-facing app UI, app showcases

Approach:
- start from user flow or interaction quality
- talk about ease, speed, familiarity, momentum, readability
- keep it lighter than enterprise SaaS writing

Tone:
- fluid
- active
- user-centered

### Mode 4 — Editorial / Story-led
Best for: culture, education, niche audience products, faith/content apps

Approach:
- open with a point of view or a framing thought
- connect the digital experience to tone, emotion, or context
- keep it restrained and grounded

Tone:
- human
- thoughtful
- not overly poetic

### Mode 5 — Compact showcase
Best for: UI shots, hero sections, bento layouts, component showcases

Approach:
- 1 strong statement
- 1 supporting line
- CTA

Tone:
- concise
- visual-first
- clean

## Post-Type Guidelines

## 1) Premium Website Post
Use for:
- real estate websites
- brand websites
- service company sites
- corporate or institutional sites
- landing pages that represent a business clearly

What to emphasize:
- clarity of presentation
- trust
- first impression
- positioning
- hierarchy
- conversion-readiness
- credibility

What not to do:
- do not write it like a software platform
- do not just call it "modern, clean, premium"
- do not sound like a brochure

Title formulas:
- `Brand — Premium Website for [Category]`
- `Brand — Website for [Audience or Industry]`
- `Brand — [Category] Website Designed for [Business Outcome]`

Caption angle ideas:
- trust-first
- clearer positioning
- better first impression
- stronger presentation of services / properties / offerings
- premium without excess

Good CTA types:
- conversation CTA
- exploration CTA
- website CTA

## Title Rules

Titles should:

- make the category obvious
- stay commercially useful
- stay readable on profile grids
- help cold viewers understand the work quickly

Titles should **not**:

- be only brand names when the brand is not famous
- read like an SEO blog headline
- become long technical slugs
- sound identical across all posts

### Best title patterns

- `Brand — Website for [Category]`
- `Brand — Dashboard for [Use Case]`
- `Brand — Mobile App for [Audience]`
- `Brand — Product Experience for [Function]`
- `Brand — Landing Page for [Business Type]`

### Title quality check
Ask:
- would a stranger understand the category?
- does this help discovery?
- does this feel premium and clean?
- is this better than just the raw brand name?

## CTA Rules

CTA objective:

- drive profile visits
- drive website visits
- create inquiry intent
- encourage exploration of related work

Main rule:
**Sell curiosity first, service second.**

### CTA style
CTAs must be:
- short
- clean
- natural
- confidence-led
- curiosity-based

CTAs must not be:
- loud
- spammy
- needy
- urgent
- discount-like
- ad-bro nonsense

### Approved CTA types

#### Exploration CTA
Use when the goal is profile discovery or website visits.

Examples:
- Explore more work on our profile.
- Visit Craftnotion to see more of our work.
- Explore more product and website work on our profile.
- More work available through the link in profile.

#### Conversation CTA
Use when the work is strong enough to trigger inquiry intent.

Examples:
- Building something similar? Let’s talk.
- Need a sharper digital experience for your brand or product? Let’s connect.
- Planning a website or product revamp? Let’s talk.

#### Case Study CTA
Use for real project showcases.

Examples:
- Explore more case studies and product work on our website.
- View more of our recent project work through our profile.

#### Niche Service CTA
Use for SaaS, websites, apps, MVPs, dashboards.

Examples:
- We design and build SaaS platforms, websites, and digital products for growing businesses.
- We help businesses build better websites, apps, and product experiences.

### CTA frequency rule
Each post should ideally have:
- 1 primary CTA
- optional 1 support line only when necessary

That’s it.

## Alt Text Rules

Alt text should describe the image.
It is not a dumping ground for keywords.
It is not a branding slogan.

### Alt text should:
- say what the image is
- mention 1–2 important visible elements
- stay short and natural
- help accessibility first

### Alt text should not:
- become a paragraph
- repeat every keyword from tags
- stuff the agency name unnaturally
- sound like a search query

### Good alt text pattern
`[type of design] for [brand] with [1–2 key visible elements]`

Examples:
- Fintech dashboard for Tredzy with LC discounting workflows and finance operations.
- Premium real estate website for Speed Infra with property sections and modern layouts.
- Learning platform dashboard for NNIIT LMS with student, teacher, and admin modules.

### Agency name rule
Include "by Craftnotion" only when it feels natural and useful.
Do not force it into every alt text.

## Tag Strategy Rules

Tags should improve relevance, not create tag soup.

### Use a 4-layer tag mix

For each post, aim for:

- **3–4 broad discoverability tags**
- **4–5 niche category tags**
- **2–3 industry/context tags**
- **1–2 service/intent tags**

### Good tag categories

#### Broad tags
Examples:
- website design
- web design
- ui design
- dashboard ui
- mobile app design
- product design

#### Niche tags
Examples:
- fintech dashboard
- real estate website
- lms ui
- expense management
- login ui
- workflow automation
- sports app

#### Industry/context tags
Examples:
- trade finance
- preschool website
- golf website
- mental wellness
- travel expense management
- korean language app

#### Service/intent tags
Examples:
- conversion design
- responsive website
- b2b saas
- landing page ui
- admin dashboard

### Tag mistakes to avoid
- duplicate phrasing that means the same thing
- useless vanity tags
- tagging every post with the agency name
- stuffing irrelevant high-volume tags
- over-tagging every possible interpretation

### Tag quality check
Ask:
- would someone actually browse this tag for this kind of work?
- is this tag relevant to what the shot visibly is?
- does the tag support discovery or just inflate the list?

## Image / Carousel Packaging Rules

### On-image text
Allowed:
- project title
- brand name
- short service label
- subtle branding
- one final slide with soft CTA when using a carousel

Avoid:
- "hire us now"
- "book now"
- "limited slots"
- cluttered promotional overlays
- stuffing CTA text into the main hero visual

### Carousel logic
If using multiple slides, structure them like this:

1. Hook / hero visual
2. Key supporting screens or sections
3. one proof / value / feature context slide if needed
4. closing brand or CTA slide only if it fits naturally

Do not turn every carousel into a mini pitch deck.

## Profile-Level Consistency Rules

Even with variation across posts, the profile should still sound like one company.

Keep these consistent across all posts:

- tone
- commercial awareness
- clarity level
- CTA quality
- title logic
- overall level of polish

Do **not** let the profile feel like:
- 10 different interns wrote captions
- one AI wrote everything in the same rhythm
- half the posts target designers and half target buyers

---

## Sequencing Rules for New Posts

When releasing a new set of posts, do not dump them randomly.

### Best posting mix
For every 8–10 posts, try to balance:

- 3 SaaS / dashboard / product posts
- 2 premium website posts
- 1 mobile app post
- 1 concept / component post
- 1 niche or industry-specific post
- 1 proof-heavy or especially commercial post

### Avoid posting in long repetitive runs like:
- 6 website posts back-to-back
- 5 dark fintech posts in a row
- 4 school projects in a row
- 8 concept shots without client relevance

The feed should feel curated, not accidental.

---

## Review Checklist Before Publishing

Before publishing a Dribbble post, check all of this:

### Positioning
- Does this support Craftnotion’s strongest lanes?
- Is the category obvious quickly?
- Does it make the studio feel more premium and focused?

### Title
- Is the title clearer than just the brand name?
- Is it readable and commercially useful?
- Does it avoid sounding stiff or bloated?

### Caption
- Does it sound human and intentional?
- Is it free from agency clichés?
- Does it match the actual shot?
- Is the rhythm right for the type of post?

### CTA
- Does the CTA feel premium?
- Does it invite instead of push?
- Is there only one real CTA?

### Alt text
- Is it short and descriptive?
- Does it describe the image instead of gaming search?

### Tags
- Are the tags relevant and intentional?
- Do they help discovery without becoming tag spam?

### Overall
- Would a founder or business owner trust this?
- Would this feel good on a premium agency profile?
- Is this better than a generic portfolio caption?
