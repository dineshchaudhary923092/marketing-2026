# LinkedIn Content Skill for a Design & Development Agency

## Skill Name

LinkedIn Post Strategy and Quality Control for Craftnotion-Type Agencies

## Purpose

Use this skill to plan, write, review, and improve LinkedIn posts for a professional design and development agency. The goal is to make the agency look credible, commercially sharp, execution-focused, and worth hiring.

This skill is designed for agencies that want LinkedIn to function as:

* a trust-building channel
* a proof-of-work channel
* a positioning channel
* a lead-generation support channel

This is not for generic motivational posting, trend-chasing, or shallow engagement bait.

---

## Core Outcome

Every LinkedIn post should do at least one of these jobs:

1. Prove capability
2. Build trust
3. Show thinking
4. Clarify positioning
5. Start relevant conversations with potential clients

If a post does none of the above, it is filler.

---

## Brand Positioning Rules

Assume the agency wants to be perceived as:

* professional
* reliable
* commercially aware
* design-sensitive
* technically strong
* outcome-oriented
* easy to work with

Do not let the agency sound like:

* a freelancer scrambling for work
* a generic social media brand
* a motivational page
* a trend repost account
* a design page obsessed only with aesthetics

The tone should feel like a serious product and engineering partner.

---

## Audience

Primary audience:

* founders
* startup operators
* SMEs
* business owners
* decision-makers looking for web, app, SaaS, MVP, redesign, or product development help

Secondary audience:

* designers
* developers
* collaborators
* referrals
* industry peers

Write for buyers first, peers second.

---

## Global LinkedIn Post Principles

These apply to every post type.

### 1. Clarity beats cleverness

Write so a founder or business owner immediately understands:

* what was done
* why it matters
* why the agency is credible

### 2. Specificity beats fluff

Avoid broad claims like:

* world-class solutions
* cutting-edge experiences
* transformative innovation
* digital excellence

Prefer:

* designed a 12-screen onboarding flow
* reduced page clutter
* improved mobile clarity
* built a scalable admin dashboard
* structured the MVP for faster launch

### 3. Commercial framing beats art-school framing

Do not talk only about colors, layouts, or aesthetics.
Connect work to business utility:

* user clarity
* trust
* conversions
* onboarding ease
* faster internal operations
* clearer product positioning

### 4. Show the thinking, not just the output

A pretty screenshot without reasoning is weak.
A screenshot with context, tradeoffs, and decisions is stronger.

### 5. One post, one job

Do not cram five messages into one post.
Each post should have one clear theme.

### 6. Write like a confident operator

The voice should be direct, grounded, and credible.
Not needy. Not theatrical. Not overhyped.

---

## The Recommended Content Mix

For a design and development agency, a healthy ongoing mix is:

* 40% project and case-study posts
* 20% process and expertise posts
* 20% trust and proof posts
* 10% founder or agency point-of-view posts
* 10% culture, milestone, or relationship posts

This balance prevents the page from becoming:

* only a gallery
* only a self-congratulation machine
* only a thought-leadership costume

---

## Posting Cadence Guidance

Recommended baseline:

* 3 posts per week

Suggested structure:

* Post 1: project or case-study post
* Post 2: process or expertise post
* Post 3: testimonial, trust, milestone, or founder POV post

If posting 2 times per week:

* 1 proof-based post
* 1 expertise-based post

If posting 4 to 5 times per week:
Keep quality high. Do not fill the feed with weak content just to stay visible.

# POST TYPES

## 1. Project Showcase Posts

### Purpose

To prove the agency builds real work for real businesses.

### Best use cases

* new launches
* redesigns
* app flows
* websites
* SaaS dashboards
* MVPs
* feature revamps
* branding-plus-product rollouts

### What the post must communicate

* what the project was
* who it was for
* what problem existed
* what the agency improved or built
* why the final solution works

### Recommended post structure

1. Hook
2. Context
3. Problem
4. Approach
5. Key output or solution
6. Outcome or takeaway
7. CTA or closing line

### Strong hook styles

* We redesigned a platform that needed to look more credible and easier to use.
* This project needed clarity, not more visual noise.
* We helped turn a rough product idea into a usable MVP.
* This app needed to feel trustworthy before it could feel impressive.

### What to include visually

* cover slide with strong title
* key screens
* before/after comparisons where possible
* short annotations
* problem-to-solution storytelling

### Do

* mention the business context
* mention what was delivered
* explain why certain design or product decisions were made
* keep the visual system consistent

### Do not

* dump random screenshots with no story
* make every showcase sound identical
* over-focus on aesthetics while ignoring business usefulness
* post weak projects that do not represent desired future work

### Quality test

A stranger should understand the project in under 10 seconds.

---

## 2. Mini Case Study Posts

### Purpose

To turn completed work into strategic proof.

### Difference from showcase posts

A showcase says what was built.
A case study says why it was built that way and what changed.

### Best use cases

* higher-value projects
* stronger clients
* complex platforms
* redesigns with clear business logic
* multi-stage engagements

### Recommended structure

1. Client or industry context
2. Initial challenge
3. Strategic decisions made
4. What was designed or developed
5. What improved
6. Key takeaway

### What makes these strong

* business framing
* practical reasoning
* clear changes
* mature presentation

### Visual guidance

* 4 to 6 carousel slides
* problem slide
* solution slide
* screens slide
* result or summary slide

### Common mistake

Turning a case study into a brag post with no substance.

---

## 3. Testimonial Posts

### Purpose

To build trust using external validation.

### Best use cases

* after a successful delivery
* after launch support
* when the quote mentions reliability, clarity, process, quality, communication, or outcomes

### Best format

Carousel, not just a single quote image.

### Recommended testimonial carousel structure

* Slide 1: hook from the testimonial
* Slide 2: full or partial quote
* Slide 3: what was delivered
* Slide 4: project visual or short project summary

### What makes a testimonial post strong

* clear source identity
* readable quote
* visible project context
* connection to actual services delivered

### What weakens it

* generic praise with no context
* too much decoration
* quote cards with no project tie-in
* overly long paragraphs with no hierarchy

### Guidelines

* prioritize quotes that mention specific strengths
* use testimonials that sound human, not sanitized
* highlight one insight per testimonial

### Best testimonial themes

* communication
* ownership
* problem-solving
* speed with care
* product thinking
* reliable delivery
* professional collaboration

---

## 4. Expertise / Educational Posts

### Purpose

To show the agency knows what it is doing.

### Best use cases

* explaining product decisions
* design mistakes businesses make
* MVP planning insights
* UX observations
* technical tradeoffs
* launch-readiness advice
* design/dev process thinking

### Recommended structure

1. Sharp opening statement
2. 3 to 5 focused insights
3. Practical close

### Good topic directions

* Why most MVPs become messy before launch
* What businesses get wrong when hiring an agency
* The difference between a pretty UI and a useful product
* Why fast onboarding flows usually convert better
* What makes a SaaS dashboard feel trustworthy
* When a business needs redesign versus restructuring

### Rules

* keep it practical
* use real-world language
* avoid sounding like a course seller
* avoid generic top-5 listicles unless the points are genuinely strong

### Do

* take a position
* explain tradeoffs
* share lessons from actual work

### Do not

* recycle vague design tips from the internet
* overuse buzzwords
* pretend every observation is revolutionary

---

## 5. Founder / Agency Point-of-View Posts

### Purpose

To give the brand a brain and a spine.

### Best use cases

* strong opinions about product quality
* honest takes on agency-client dynamics
* lessons from building and shipping
* decisions around scope, process, or execution

### Why these matter

They make the agency feel led, not assembled.

### Tone rules

* direct
* grounded
* not whiny
* not arrogant
* not fake-deep

### Good topic directions

* Why cheap development often becomes expensive later
* Why most redesign requests are actually clarity problems
* Why clients should judge agencies by thinking, not noise
* Why speed matters, but structure matters more
* What experienced founders usually understand earlier

### Danger zone

These can easily become self-righteous. Keep them useful.

---

## 6. Process Posts

### Purpose

To show how the agency works, not just what it produces.

### Best use cases

* discovery process
* wireframing logic
* UX audit approach
* dev handoff clarity
* QA thinking
* sprint planning
* feature prioritization

### What these posts should achieve

They reduce buyer uncertainty.
They answer: what will it feel like to work with this agency?

### Recommended angle types

* our redesign process
* how we structure MVPs
* how we decide what not to build in phase one
* how we turn requirements into shipping plans

### Strong closing line examples

* Good work gets easier when the process is clear.
* The goal is not to add more screens. The goal is to reduce confusion.
* Better products usually come from better decisions made earlier.

---

## 7. Before / After Posts

### Purpose

To make improvement visible fast.

### Best use cases

* landing pages
* hero sections
* dashboards
* onboarding screens
* mobile app UI cleanup
* brand refreshes

### Why these work

They are easy to understand and visually immediate.

### Rules

* make the difference obvious
* annotate what changed
* explain why the new version works better
* do not rely only on visuals; add reasoning

### What to mention

* hierarchy
* readability
* trust
* clarity
* conversion logic
* usability improvements

---

## 8. Launch / Milestone Posts

### Purpose

To show momentum and legitimacy.

### Best use cases

* website launch
* app launch
* new feature release
* major redesign completion
* team or company milestone

### Make these stronger by adding

* what was launched
* why it matters
* who it helps
* what the engagement involved

### Avoid

* empty celebration language
* “excited to announce” with no real information

### Better than generic launch language

* We recently launched a platform designed to make X simpler for Y.
* This release focused on clarity, trust, and faster user action.

---

## 9. Trust and Social Proof Posts

### Purpose

To reinforce that the agency has done meaningful work with real people.

### Formats

* testimonials
* client feedback snippets
* delivery highlights
* case-study proof frames
* project summaries with quote snippets

### Best practice

Anchor proof to something tangible:

* an industry
* a product type
* a problem solved
* a deliverable

### Weak version

Client loved working with us.

### Stronger version

We helped a fintech founder shape and improve their product experience, and their feedback highlighted our communication, speed, and ownership.

---

## 10. Opinionated Industry Posts

### Purpose

To make the brand memorable and worth following.

### Best use cases

* poor product habits in the market
* common mistakes in websites or apps
* buyer misconceptions
* startup mistakes around scope, speed, and UX

### Rules

* have a real point
* keep it constructive
* do not attack people for attention
* avoid lazy contrarianism

### Good structure

* sharp claim
* brief reasoning
* example or pattern
* practical takeaway

---

## 11. Team / Culture Posts

### Purpose

To humanize the agency without turning the page into an internal scrapbook.

### Best use cases

* meaningful behind-the-scenes moments
* design sprints
* build days
* product reviews
* milestone celebrations with context

### Rules

* only post what supports credibility or chemistry
* keep it professional
* avoid low-signal filler

### Culture content should support one of these impressions

* thoughtful team
* committed process
* genuine collaboration
* disciplined execution

---

## 12. Hiring / Collaboration Posts

### Purpose

To attract the right collaborators while keeping the brand professional.

### Rules

* state exactly what is needed
* say what kind of work the agency does
* keep standards clear
* avoid desperate tone

# WRITING GUIDELINES

## Hook Rules

The first line decides whether people keep reading.
A hook should do one of these:

* create curiosity
* state a sharp insight
* expose a problem
* promise a useful breakdown
* show a result or lesson

### Weak hooks

* We are excited to share
* Another great project we completed
* At Craftnotion, we believe
* Design is very important

### Better hooks

* Most redesign requests are actually clarity problems.
* This project needed trust before it needed polish.
* We helped simplify a product that was doing too much at once.
* Not every MVP needs more features. Some need fewer decisions.

---

## Body Writing Rules

* keep paragraphs short
* use whitespace for readability
* avoid jargon unless necessary
* avoid over-explaining obvious points
* make each sentence earn its place

---

## CTA Rules

LinkedIn CTAs should feel light and intelligent.
Do not beg for engagement.

### Better CTA examples

* Curious how others approach this.
* This is the kind of work we enjoy solving.
* A lot of product issues start here.
* If your platform feels harder than it should, this is often why.

### Avoid

* Like, share, and comment
* DM us now for best services
* Follow for more tips

---

# VISUAL GUIDELINES

## General Visual Rules

* use clean, premium layouts
* maintain consistent typography
* avoid clutter
* build clear hierarchy
* ensure slides are readable on mobile
* use branded but restrained styling

## For carousels

* one idea per slide
* keep text limited and readable
* do not overload with tiny body copy
* use visual rhythm across slides
* ensure the first slide is strong enough to stop scrolling

## For screenshots/mockups

* avoid random collage chaos
* curate the strongest views
* use simple annotations where helpful
* show relevance, not just quantity

## For testimonials

* use one primary statement per slide
* connect quote to a real project where possible
* remove gimmicky fake UI elements unless they serve a purpose

# WHAT TO AVOID ACROSS ALL LINKEDIN POSTS

Do not post:

* generic motivational quotes
* festival greetings too often
* recycled design trends with no original thinking
* weak projects that damage positioning
* random team pictures with no context
* too much self-praise
* language that sounds inflated or fake-premium
* captions full of adjectives but no substance

Avoid these phrases unless there is a strong reason:

* thrilled to announce
* cutting-edge
* innovative solutions
* game-changing
* seamless experiences
* world-class
* digital transformation experts

These phrases are not banned because they are evil.
They are banned because they are exhausted.

# CONTENT QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before publishing any LinkedIn post, check:

## Strategy

* Does this support the agency’s positioning?
* Does this attract the type of client we want?
* Does this make us look more credible?

## Clarity

* Can someone understand the post quickly?
* Is the message obvious?
* Is there one main takeaway?

## Proof

* Is there actual substance here?
* Is there context, reasoning, or evidence?
* Would this impress a buyer, not just a designer?

## Writing

* Is the hook strong?
* Are there filler phrases that should be removed?
* Is the tone confident but not inflated?

## Visuals

* Is slide 1 scroll-stopping?
* Is the text readable on mobile?
* Is the design polished but not overdone?

If a post fails two or more of these checks, rework it.

---

# BATCHING SYSTEM

Use this weekly system to avoid random posting.

## Step 1: Pick 3 post goals for the week

For example:

* prove product capability
* show process maturity
* reinforce trust

## Step 2: Choose one post for each goal

Example:

* case study
* process breakdown
* testimonial carousel

## Step 3: Align visuals

Make sure the week does not look visually repetitive.

## Step 4: Review balance

Avoid posting three testimonials in a row or three hot takes in a row.

---

# REPURPOSING RULES

One strong project can become multiple LinkedIn posts:

* project showcase
* mini case study
* before/after post
* testimonial post tied to the project
* process post about one decision inside the project
* founder POV post about a lesson from the engagement

This avoids content drought without forcing low-quality ideas.

---

# COMMENT AND ENGAGEMENT RULES

Posting is not enough.
The agency should act alive.

## After posting

* reply to comments with substance
* avoid one-word replies
* keep tone professional and human
* add clarity where relevant

## Engagement approach

* comment on founder and business content in a useful way
* avoid spammy networking behavior
* write comments that sound like a competent operator, not a lead scraper

---

# POSITIONING FILTER

Before posting anything, ask:
Would a serious founder see this and think:

* these people know what they’re doing
* these people ship real work
* these people understand business context
* these people look reliable to work with

If the answer is no, do not post it.

---

# FINAL RULE

The goal of LinkedIn is not to look busy.
The goal is to look credible, sharp, and consistently worth noticing.

A professional design and development agency should use LinkedIn to create the impression that:

* it builds real things
* it thinks clearly
* it communicates well
* it can be trusted with serious work

Anything that weakens that impression is noise.
