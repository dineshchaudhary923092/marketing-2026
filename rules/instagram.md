# Skill: Craftnotion Social Media Packaging & Post Guidelines

## Purpose

Use this skill to create, review, and refine Facebook and Instagram posts for Craftnotion so that every post improves one or more of the following:

* brand clarity
* trust
* authority
* engagement
* conversion intent

This skill is for a premium design and development agency profile. The goal is not to look busy. The goal is to look reliable, proven, and worth contacting.

---

## Core Objective

Every post must help a visitor reach one of these conclusions quickly:

1. Craftnotion builds serious digital products.
2. Craftnotion understands business problems, not just visuals.
3. Craftnotion is trustworthy, structured, and safe to hire.
4. Craftnotion has real proof of delivery.
5. Craftnotion is active, modern, and credible.

If a post does not strengthen at least one of the above, it should not be posted.

---

## Universal Rules For All Posts

### Rule 1: Every post needs one job

A post should do one primary job:

* showcase work
* build trust
* educate
* answer objections
* create inquiry intent

Do not make one post try to do everything.

### Rule 2: The first slide or first frame must be instantly clear

Within 1 to 2 seconds, the viewer should understand:

* what the post is about
* why they should care
* why it is relevant to business/product building

No vague hooks.
No artsy confusion.
No over-clever nonsense.

### Rule 3: Design cannot overpower the message

Premium visuals are good.
Decorative chaos is not.
The message must always win.

### Rule 4: Clarity beats cleverness

Avoid generic agency lines like:

* turning ideas into reality
* crafting digital experiences
* innovation meets design
* redefining excellence

These say nothing.

### Rule 5: Every post should sound like a real operator wrote it

Tone should feel:

* confident
* practical
* modern
* premium
* grounded

Not too corporate.
Not too playful.
Not preachy.
Not motivational fluff.

### Rule 6: Use consistent service language

Approved service labels:

* SaaS Development
* Web Application Development
* Mobile App Development
* Business Website Design & Development
* UI/UX Design
* Product Strategy
* Ongoing Support & Maintenance

Do not keep inventing new service terms per post.

### Rule 7: Each post must contain a reason to continue

That reason can be:

* useful insight
* proof
* visual payoff
* process clarity
* result
* practical takeaway

If there is no reason to continue to slide 2, the post is weak.

### Rule 8: Avoid overuse of hard-sell CTAs

Most posts should end with a light CTA, not a desperate sales pitch.
Good CTA examples:

* Planning something similar?
* Want a clearer product process?
* Building a website or app this year?
* Need a dependable product team?

Avoid sounding like an ad on every post.

## POST TYPE GUIDELINES

# 1. PROJECT POSTS

## Goal

Project posts should show what Craftnotion built and why it matters.
They are not just portfolio drops.
They must connect visuals to business value.

## Primary outcomes

* capability
* relevance
* authority
* interest

## Best format

Carousel

## Ideal structure

### Slide 1

Project hook
Examples:

* Built for speed, clarity, and scale
* A premium website for a growing brand
* From idea to launch: a modern product build
* What we built for [industry/client type]

### Slide 2

Project overview

* client type
* business context
* what was needed

### Slide 3

What we delivered
Use 3 to 5 short points only.

### Slide 4

Key strength or differentiator
Examples:

* cleaner user journeys
* improved trust and premium perception
* clear structure for growth
* better admin and content workflows
* strong mobile responsiveness

### Slide 5

Optional CTA

* Planning something similar?
* Need a custom build for your business?

## Design rules

* Slide 1 must be bold and simple
* Do not put long paragraphs on the cover
* Use real screenshots when possible
* Avoid mockup overload
* Show the product, not only typography

## Copy rules

* focus on problem + solution
* explain what matters
* avoid empty praise about your own work

## What not to do

* generic showcase with no context
* too many feature bullets
* no explanation of why the work matters
* purely visual post with no business framing

---

# 2. TESTIMONIAL POSTS

## Goal

Testimonial posts are trust assets.
Their job is to make potential clients think:
These people are reliable and safe to hire.

## Best format

3-slide carousel

## Ideal structure

### Slide 1: Hook quote

Use one derived line from the testimonial.
This slide must be short.
Structure:

* short quote
* client name
* role/company
* optional small service label

### Slide 2: Full testimonial

* full testimonial text
* client name
* role/company
* clean reading layout

### Slide 3: What we delivered

* title: What We Delivered
* project type
* service/scope
* timeline/support/context
* one short bottom line

## Design rules

* current testimonial image should usually become slide 2, not slide 1
* cover must be skimmable
* full quote should be readable, not decorative
* keep backgrounds supportive, not dominant

## Copy rules

Slide 1 hook types:

* speed
* communication
* professionalism
* ownership
* responsiveness
* long-term support

Slide 3 should be proof-first, not sales-first.
It should add context, not repeat the quote.

## What not to do

* single slide with huge paragraph and no hook
* repeating testimonial language on all three slides
* making slide 3 another ad
* posting vague praise with no business context

# 5. INFO / AUTHORITY POSTS

## Goal

Make viewers save and share the content while seeing Craftnotion as knowledgeable.

## Best format

Carousel

## Best topics

* Website vs web app vs mobile app
* What usually increases software cost
* Biggest mistakes founders make before building an MVP
* What affects project timelines
* Why unclear requirements delay delivery
* What clients should prepare before starting a product build

## Ideal structure

### Slide 1

Strong practical hook
Examples:

* What usually makes software projects expensive
* Website vs web app vs mobile app: what actually fits your business?
* 5 mistakes founders make before building an MVP

### Slide 2 onward

Each slide = one point
Short heading
Short explanation

### Last slide

Summary / takeaway / light CTA

## Design rules

* keep each slide lightweight
* avoid too much text
* use clear hierarchy
* make the save value obvious

## Copy rules

* be useful, not preachy
* speak like someone who has seen projects go wrong before
* use plain language

## What not to do

* motivational quotes
* inspirational founder filler
* generic business tips unrelated to your services

* weak trust
* cluttered layout

* unclear CTA

### Slide 3

Examples:

* clearer messaging
* stronger trust
* better navigation

* show a real contrast
* do not over-explain the obvious
* keep the transformation visual and verbal

## CAPTION GUIDELINES

### Instagram captions

Should be:

* tighter
* more scroll-friendly
* keyword aware
* clear on value

### Recommended structure

1. Hook line
2. 1 to 3 short support lines
3. light CTA

### Example caption rhythm

We built this [project type] for [client/business type].
The focus was on [clarity / trust / usability / speed].
From structure to final execution, every part was designed to support real business goals.
Planning something similar?

### Facebook captions

Should be:

* slightly more detailed
* more contextual
* still readable

### Recommended structure

1. what the project/post is about
2. why it matters
3. key details or takeaway
4. CTA

### Hashtag guidelines

Use a small relevant set.
Do not spam.
Examples:

* #WebDevelopment
* #SaaS
* #UIUX
* #AppDevelopment
* #BusinessWebsite
* #CustomSoftware

Use only those relevant to the post.

* strong first-slide hierarchy
* less text on slide 1
* sharper design discipline
* polished mockups/screens
* consistent brand palette

### Facebook visual rules

* same quality
* slightly more explanatory structure

## BAD POST TYPES TO AVOID

Do not post these unless there is a very strong strategic reason:

* generic motivational quotes
* festival posts unless essential for brand relationship reasons
* random office filler
* “innovation” wallpaper posts
* low-context design-only posts
* heavy text cover slides
* vague industry observations with no takeaway

---

## APPROVED CTA STYLES

Keep CTA language light and premium.

Examples:

* Planning something similar?
* Need a dependable team for your next build?
* Building a product or website this year?
* Looking for a clearer development process?
* Want to discuss your next project?

Avoid aggressive lines like:

* DM now
* hurry up
* limited slots
* don’t miss out

This is an agency, not a flash-sale sofa warehouse.

---

## QUALITY CONTROL CHECKLIST

Before approving any post, check:

1. Is the first slide instantly clear?
2. Does this post have one main job?
3. Does it strengthen trust, authority, engagement, or conversion?
4. Is the text readable at feed size?
5. Is the design supporting the message, not fighting it?
6. Does it sound like a serious premium agency?
7. Would a potential client learn something useful or feel more confident after seeing it?
8. Is the CTA light and appropriate?
9. Does the post match Craftnotion’s broader packaging?
10. If this post was the first thing someone saw, would it help or hurt perception?

If the post fails 2 or more of these, revise it.

---

## POST REVIEW FRAMEWORK

When reviewing any draft, score it on:

* clarity
* trust
* relevance
* visual discipline
* usefulness
* premium feel
* conversion support

Out of 10.
If any key metric is below 7, improve it before posting.
