# Skill: Craftnotion X Content Guidelines

## Purpose

Use this skill to create, review, and refine X posts for Craftnotion.

X should position Craftnotion as a sharp, credible, product-aware team with real opinions and real delivery maturity.

The account should feel like:

* builders
* operators
* product thinkers
* people who understand what goes wrong in websites, apps, SaaS, and digital product execution

The account should not feel like:

* a portfolio feed in text form
* corporate filler
* a quote machine
* a generic agency account chasing visibility with hollow noise

---

## Platform Role

X is the public thinking layer of the brand.

If Instagram is the showroom and Facebook is the credibility checkpoint, X is the place where Craftnotion proves it can think clearly in public.

X should help people conclude:

1. these people know product and execution deeply
2. these people have strong judgment
3. these people are not just designers or coders, but problem-solvers
4. these people would probably be good to work with

---

## Primary Objective

Each X post should do one of these well:

* share a sharp product or business observation
* show real-world execution maturity
* build trust through honest insight
* make Craftnotion feel worth following
* create curiosity about the team’s work and thinking

If a post cannot do one of those, it is likely not worth posting.

---

## Core Difference Between X and Threads

Threads can be softer, more human, and more observational.

X should be:

* tighter
* sharper
* more pointed
* more insight-dense
* more deliberate

Threads can feel conversational.
X should feel like controlled public thinking.

---

## Voice Rules

### Tone should be

* clear
* sharp
* credible
* compact
* practical
* slightly opinionated

### Tone should not be

* arrogant
* preachy
* too playful
* over-explained
* motivational guru style
* startup-bro cringe

### Golden rule

Write like someone who has built enough things to know where theory breaks.

---

## What X Is Best For

X works well for:

* sharp takes
* compact lessons
* product and design judgment
* client misconceptions
* execution truths
* mini case studies
* tactical observations
* public thinking around launches, UX, websites, SaaS, scope, and product strategy

X is weak for:

* giant generic announcements
* bland “we are thrilled” copy
* purely visual flexing with no insight
* repetitive promotional posting

---

## Core Content Pillars

### 1. Sharp Observations

Short truths about product, websites, apps, or agency work.

Examples:

* Most websites do not suffer from too little animation. They suffer from unclear messaging.
* Scope problems often begin in assumptions, not in development.
* A premium UI cannot rescue a confused workflow.

### 2. Execution Lessons

What real delivery work teaches.

Examples:

* Speed in software is rarely about coding faster. It is usually about reducing decision friction.
* Better requirements often outperform bigger teams.
* Most admin panels are overbuilt and underthought.

### 3. Judgment Posts

Opinion-backed statements that show discernment.

Examples:

* A cheap quote is often just expensive ambiguity delayed.
* If your homepage tries to say everything, it usually says nothing well.
* Not every product idea needs an app first.

### 4. Proof-Backed Insight

Use project experience without sounding self-congratulatory.

Examples:

* One thing that consistently helps projects move faster: fewer unclear decision-makers.
* Some of the strongest client feedback we get is about clarity and responsiveness, not just output.
* A lot of our work is really product cleanup before product execution.

### 5. Mini Frameworks

Compact tactical frameworks perform well.

Examples:

* 3 things that make websites feel instantly more trustworthy
* 4 signals that a product needs structural clarity before design polish
* 5 mistakes founders make before building an MVP

## Approved Post Types

# 1. ONE-LINE TAKES

## Purpose

Fast, memorable insight.

## Examples

* Premium is usually more about restraint than decoration.
* A slow project often has a decision problem, not a dev problem.
* Good UX often looks simple because the hard thinking already happened.

## Rules

* should feel sharp and repostable
* no filler words
* no vague inspiration

---

# 2. TWO-TO-FIVE LINE INSIGHT POSTS

## Purpose

Expand a point slightly while keeping it native to X.

## Structure

* strong opening line
* explanation
* consequence or takeaway

## Example

A lot of businesses ask for a redesign when they actually need a clearer message hierarchy.
If users do not understand what you do quickly, better visuals alone will not fix much.
Trust drops before design gets a chance to impress.

## Rules

* keep it compact
* make every line earn its place

---

# 3. MINI THREADS

## Purpose

Deliver more value when a single post is not enough.

## Good use cases

* frameworks
* lessons from projects
* breakdowns of common mistakes
* product thinking sequences

## Recommended size

3 to 7 posts max

## Example structures

### Framework thread

Post 1: hook
Post 2: point 1
Post 3: point 2
Post 4: point 3
Post 5: takeaway

### Mistake thread

Post 1: common mistake
Post 2: why it happens
Post 3: what it causes
Post 4: better way

## Rules

* thread should justify itself
* do not turn simple ideas into fake long-form content
* every post must add something

---

# 4. CONTRARIAN / CLARITY POSTS

## Purpose

Show judgment by challenging weak assumptions.

## Examples

* Not every business needs an app. Some need a clearer backend workflow first.
* More features rarely fix weak product positioning.
* “Simple website” projects are often only simple on paper.

## Rules

* challenge ideas, not people
* stay practical
* avoid edgy nonsense for attention

---

# 5. CLIENT MISCONCEPTION POSTS

## Purpose

Educate while showing real-world experience.

## Examples

* Clients often think delays start in development. Many start in unclear priorities.
* Founders sometimes ask for “just an MVP” when the actual workflow is still undefined.
* Many people underestimate how much trust a weak website quietly destroys.

## Rules

* useful, not mocking
* write with empathy
* speak from pattern recognition

---

# 6. MINI CASE STUDY POSTS

## Purpose

Translate project work into native X content.

## Structure

* situation
* what mattered most
* what changed
* takeaway

## Example

Worked on a project recently where the main issue was not the interface.
It was trust.
The business looked less established online than it really was.
Once messaging, hierarchy, and structure improved, the whole experience felt more credible.

## Rules

* focus on lesson
* avoid heavy self-promotion
* do not write like an award submission

---

# 7. QUESTION POSTS

## Purpose

Start useful conversations.

## Good formats

* What damages trust faster on a website: weak messaging or weak structure?
* What is one thing founders underestimate before building software?
* What makes a business website feel premium to you?

## Rules

* questions must be relevant
* avoid low-signal bait
* ask things professionals can actually answer thoughtfully

---

# 8. FRAMEWORK POSTS

## Purpose

Provide reusable thinking.

## Examples

* 3 ways to make a business website feel more trustworthy
* 4 signs a SaaS MVP is trying to do too much
* 5 things that quietly increase software cost

## Rules

* framework must be useful
* points should be distinct
* avoid bloated lists

---

# 9. LIGHTWEIGHT PROOF POSTS

## Purpose

Build trust in a subtle way.

## Examples

* A lot of our strongest client relationships are built on direct communication, not just output.
* Some of the fastest projects we have delivered were also the clearest, not the simplest.
* We often end up solving structural problems before we even start designing.

## Rules

* proof should feel woven into insight
* do not brag loudly
* avoid repeating stats too often

---

## Posting Philosophy

On X, a strong post should feel like a thought worth sharing, not a campaign asset.

Think:

* signal > polish
* insight > announcement
* judgment > decoration
* clarity > hype

---

## Repurposing Rules

### From Instagram to X

Never dump carousel captions into X.

Extract:

* the key observation
* the project lesson
* the business implication
* the execution truth

### From Facebook to X

Reduce explanation.
Make it tighter and more pointed.

### From Projects to X

Do not lead with:

* excited to share
* check out our latest project
* we are proud to announce

Better:

* One interesting thing about this build...
* The real challenge in this project was...
* What mattered most here was...
* A common mistake in projects like this is...

## Formatting Rules

* use line breaks well
* do not cram giant paragraphs
* keep the opening line strong
* avoid emoji clutter
* use punctuation with discipline
* avoid hashtag dependency

### Hashtags

Use rarely.
X posts should stand on their own.
If used, keep them minimal and highly relevant.

---

## CTA Rules

Most X posts should not have a hard CTA.
The content itself should carry the value.

Good CTA styles:

* Curious how others see this.
* We see this often in product work.
* This comes up more than people expect.
* Worth thinking about before the next build.
* Planning something similar?

Avoid:

* book a call now
* DM immediately
* limited slots
* follow for more tips

X audiences punish obvious thirst.

---

## What To Avoid Completely

* motivational fluff
* generic startup advice
* vague celebration posts
* overlong explanations of simple points
* trying to sound viral
* corporate tone with no personality
* thought leadership clichés
* forced controversy

---

## Review Checklist

Before publishing, check:

1. Is there one strong idea here?
2. Does it show judgment or experience?
3. Is it tighter than a Threads version would be?
4. Would a founder, product person, or business owner find this worth reading?
5. Does it help Craftnotion look credible?
6. Is the opening line strong enough?
7. Does it avoid agency cliché language?
8. Would it still work without a link or image?

If it fails 2 or more, rewrite it.

---

## Quality Standard

A strong Craftnotion X post should feel:

* sharp
* compact
* thoughtful
* high-signal
* credible
* grounded in real work

The account should feel like a product and execution mind at work in public.
Not like a company trying too hard to sound interesting.
